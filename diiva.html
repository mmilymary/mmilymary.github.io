Diiva's Juurileipä

Ordering sourdough or booking a table should feel as easy as walking in. A redesign concept for a lovely, one-of-a-kind café, bakery and restaurant in Mänttä.

// THE STARTING POINT

A lovely place, a heavy website.

Diiva is a warm café, bakery and restaurant where everything is baked by hand. The old website had all the information – but customers had to work to find it.

  • 01Ordering bread ahead and booking a table weren't clear, simple flows.
  • 02The look didn't match the rest of the brand.
  • 03Long texts. On the web, people skim – they don't read paragraphs.

// WHAT I DID

Three principles.

01

Simplify the two main tasks

Ordering bread and booking a table became short, step-by-step flows: pick, fill in, done – with a clear confirmation and a pickup code.

02

Look like the brand

A refreshed visual style that matches the café's own identity, so the website feels like the same place you walk into.

03

Write for skimming

I cut the copy down to what customers actually scan: short headings, key facts first, no jargon.

// BEFORE → AFTER

Same café, a new first impression.

“Let's sell a feeling.”

// FOR CUSTOMERS · MOBILE

Order, book, done.

// FOR THE STAFF · DASHBOARD

The other side of the counter.

A good ordering flow only works if the bakery can keep up. I designed a simple dashboard where the owner sees today's orders and tables at a glance, sets how many loaves are available each day, and manages lunch, evenings, seasonal menus and catering requests.

…and in your pocket.The same dashboard works on the phone, so the owner can check today's pickups or update the seasonal menu between batches.

// WHAT I LEARNED

Design for both sides.

  • A service has more than one user. Every customer feature needed a matching tool for the staff.
  • Less text makes a stronger brand. Cutting copy was harder – and more valuable – than adding it.
  • Designing for a real business means real constraints: opening hours, baking capacity, a small team.
